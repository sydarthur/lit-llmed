## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates causal effect of reimbursement policies on telemedicine adoption
- Shows adoption concentrated in inpatient vs outpatient services

## Methodology

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences with staggered rollout
- **Data**: Hospital survey data 2014-2019 across multiple states
- **Key Finding**: Reimbursement policies effective but with service-specific limitations

## Critical Notes

**Strengths**:
- Rigorous causal inference approach
- Leverages natural experiment from phased policy rollout
- Addresses endogeneity concerns with institutional theory lens

**Limitations**:
- Limited to 2014-2019 timeframe (pre-pandemic)
- May not generalize to post-COVID telemedicine landscape
- Self-reported adoption data could have measurement error

**Questions/Extensions**:
- How did COVID-19 pandemic accelerate or change these adoption patterns?
- What about quality of telemedicine services adopted?
- Economic outcomes for hospitals and patients?
